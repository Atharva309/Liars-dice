Six ways to learn to bluff

Liar's Dice is a game of hidden dice and confident lies. We built six AIs that each learn it by a different method, trained them twice, and made them play each other tens of thousands of times. This is their story in four short chapters. Then you can sit down and play them.

The game in 30 seconds

Everything the bots learn comes down to these three rules.

Everyone hides their dice

Each player rolls under a cup. You see your own dice and nobody else's.

Claim, and keep raising

A claim is about every die on the table, counted together. Each one must beat the last. You can claim more dice than you hold, and you can lie.

Or call the last claim

Meet the players

Six learners, each built on a different idea from AI research, and two hand-written bots to measure them against.

Chapter 1 of 4Next: how they trained

Nobody taught them a strategy

Every learner started out making random claims. Each then played hundreds of thousands of rounds and adjusted itself a little after every one. This is the loop they all ran.

Sit down with a crowd

Each practice round seats the learner with a mix of opponents: copies of itself, the earlier version of every bot, and the Math Bot. Beating only yourself teaches bad habits.

Read the table

The bot sees a shortlist of up to 24 claims and the chance each is true. That chance moves with the bidding: someone who keeps claiming fives probably holds some.

Get one number back

When the round ends, the bot is told how much its chance of winning the whole game just moved. Losing a die hurts more the fewer you have left.

Adjust, and repeat

The bot nudges itself toward whatever tends to raise that number, then plays again. How it does the nudging is the one thing that differs between them.

Chapter 3 of 4Next: the exam

Then we set them an exam

Win rates only say how the bots do against each other. If they all share a bad habit, nobody punishes it and the numbers still look fine. So we wrote a test with right answers. It has two papers, and both are worked out exactly instead of estimated from games.

Two papers

Paper B: the hunter

For each bot we worked out the best possible counter-strategy against that one bot, knowing all of its habits. Nothing can beat the bot by more. A bot with no exploitable habits would hold the hunter to an even share.

Paper B: the hunter

Two leaks to try

Both come straight from the hunter's plan, and both work at the table when it is down to you and one bot with 2 dice each.

The Exploiter always raises

Open with two of a low number you do not hold: ones, twos or threes. It answers with three of the same number every time, whatever it holds. With only its own 2 dice to count on, that cannot be true. Call it.

Gut Playerat least 1 twoYouLiar!
The Gut Player opens with a number it lacks

When it makes the first claim of the round, it names a number it does not hold. Call Liar unless you hold that number yourself. If you do hold it, the claim is true, so raise instead.

    Take a seat

    1. Everyone hides their dice under a cup. You only see your own.
    2. Claim or challenge. Turns go clockwise. On your turn, claim how many dice of one number are on the whole table, counting every cup, like "at least 3 fives". Each claim must beat the last one. Or call Liar! on the claim before you.

    Dice each

    How to play

    1. Hidden dice. Each player rolls their dice under a cup: 2, 3 or 5 each, your choice. You see only yours, on the rack in front of you.
    2. A claim is about every die on the table. "At least 3 fives" means: counting all cups together, three or more dice show a five. You don't need to hold them yourself, which is why you can claim more dice than you own.
    3. Raise it. A new claim must be higher: more dice ("3 fives" to "4 twos"), or the same count with a higher number ("3 fives" to "3 sixes").
    4. Or call Liar! on the claim just before yours. All cups lift and the dice are counted.
    5. Someone loses a die, and nobody ever gains one. Claim true: the caller loses one. Claim false: the claimer loses one. No dice left and you're out. Last player in wins.
    6. Rule of thumb. Each hidden die shows any given number 1 time in 6. Divide the hidden dice by 6 to see how many of a number to expect out there, then add your own.
